# PUNJABI_CANADIAN_FOOD_CATALOGUE_V2.md
# Punjabi Canadian food catalogue — owner review copy

Version 2.0 | Updated 2026-10-09 (America/Toronto). 293 unchanged foods across 16 categories.
Companion seed: PUNJABI_CANADIAN_FOOD_CATALOGUE_V2.json.

This is a broad starter menu for discovery, not a claim that every Punjabi household eats every item. It combines familiar Punjabi cooking, Canadian meals, takeout, and occasional foods. Cultural research supports varied traditional and Western food practices; the older qualitative studies do not measure current nationwide prevalence. Current Canadian food-product and GTA menu sources help check availability.

The companion JSON provides stable IDs, search aliases, portion labels, and briefs for artwork matching the current app. The artwork still needs to be implemented in the app. Nutrient numbers are deliberately absent until a source, exact label, or household recipe/portion is confirmed. Choosing a food name alone cannot establish calories or hidden oil.

Use this as All foods. Each person chooses their usual foods/favorites; the app should not put 293 items on the home screen. Vegetarian, egg, meat/fish, allergies, and specific-day preferences are individual choices, not assumptions about identity.

## Implementation notes

- Keep the current illustrated cream tiles and picker layout.
- Search aliases accommodate names such as roti/chapati/phulka, daal/dal, dahi/curd, sabji/sabzi and cha/chai. Add reviewed language spellings rather than guessing translations.
- A bowl, cup, piece or scoop is not a universal mass. Confirm the usual serving once, then reuse it.
- Tea sugar/milk, paratha filling/fat, recipe oil, package brands and serving sizes are modifiers or sourced versions.
- Distinguish incorporated recipe fat from added ghee to avoid double counting.
- Keep unknown nutrition visibly unknown. Preserve recipe versions and historical totals.
- Include Create a meal for regional, household, or Canadian items not listed.
- No food is presented as a moral good/bad choice or a recommendation that it be eaten.

## Research references and their purpose

- [punjabi families study](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0017896910373031): Qualitative Canadian family research supports varied traditional and western food practices. It does not establish the prevalence of each item here.
- [punjabi men study](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1467-9566.2010.01252.x): Context on Punjabi staples and immigrant food practices; small specific population, not all households.
- [brars gta menu](https://www.restaurants.brars.com/): Current GTA availability examples for parathas, paneer, chana, saag and related foods; not an endorsed diet or a nutrient source.
- [nanak products](https://nanakfoods.com/products): Canadian/South Asian product-discovery context for dairy, snacks and sweets; exact package labels still needed.
- [canadian nutrient file](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/food-nutrition/healthy-eating/nutrient-data/canadian-nutrient-file-about-us.html): Primary future nutrient-mapping source for Canadian foods; no nutrient values have been copied into this seed.
- [cnf 2026](https://open.canada.ca/data/en/dataset/1b6139bd-ed7e-4043-bc28-ff00e10f3109): Current downloadable dataset reference. Verify licence and source IDs during import.
- [tim hortons labels](https://help.timhortons.ca/hc/en-ca/articles/35736786288155-Where-can-I-find-ingredient-information-for-your-menu-items): Use exact official Canadian menu/size/ingredient records for branded items; generic names here carry no brand nutrient claims.

This list is curated product content. The sources inform scope and availability; they do not validate every household recipe, nutrient value, allergy status, or item frequency.
